Dictation that never leaves your Mac.

Hold a key, speak, and clean, punctuated text appears wherever you're typing. Speech recognition and cleanup both run on your Mac's own chips: no cloud, no account, and it works offline. It powers FreeFlow, the free and open-source dictation app.

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayushpatnaikgit/freeflow-local/main/get.sh | sh

Paste into Terminal. Needs an Apple Silicon Mac on macOS 14 or later, 16 GB of RAM and about 6 GB of disk. Install FreeFlow first.

What you get

Accurate speech-to-text

Orukeet, a multilingual fine-tune of NVIDIA Parakeet, running on the Apple Neural Engine in about 0.1–0.2 s per dictation.

Reads like you typed it

Google's Gemma 4 removes fillers and false starts, fixes punctuation, formats emails and uses your custom vocabulary.

Spells what's on screen

Names and terms visible in the window you're dictating into are spelled right, even when misheard: "Laura" becomes "LoRA".

Light on memory

Models load when you start speaking and unload after 10 idle minutes. Open source under Apache 2.0.

The backend is a small local server that speaks the same API FreeFlow uses for cloud services, so FreeFlow needs no changes. It listens only on your own machine (127.0.0.1).

M4 Mac, 24 GB, 6 s of speech. The first dictation after a restart takes a few seconds more; long dictations take longer because the whole text is rewritten. Switch models with freeflowctl model e2b: faster, but it follows instructions less well.

Screen words

Speech recognition turns rare words into common ones. With FreeFlow's Screen Recording permission, the backend reads the active window with Apple's on-device text recognition, picks out unusual words (names, acronyms, technical terms) and gives the cleanup model only the ones that sound like something you said.

  • In tests, misheard on-screen names and terms were fixed 13 times out of 16, against 7 without it, with no change on 77 other dictations.
  • Best for names and terms misheard as other words. For code-like tokens and words you use often, add them to FreeFlow's custom vocabulary.
  • Optional: turn it off with freeflowctl screen-words off or by not granting Screen Recording.

Privacy

  • Audio, transcripts, screenshots and cleaned text are processed on your Mac and not sent anywhere.
  • Screenshots and the words read from them stay in memory, are never written to disk, and are discarded after each dictation.
  • Logs contain timings and counts, never what you said or what was on screen.
  • Models are downloaded once from Hugging Face; after that it runs offline.

Where this is going

Neural Engine first

Run as much as possible on the Neural Engine: speech already does; next the screenshot reading and, as Apple's on-device tools mature, the cleanup model itself.

Much faster with newer models

The cleanup model is one setting. Every new candidate is measured on the same test dictations before it becomes the default, with long dictations the main target.

Fine-tuned for dictation

A small adapter trained on FreeFlow's exact requests, so a smaller, faster model can match today's quality. Trained on synthetic and public data only.

Commands

~/Library/Application\ Support/FreeFlowLocal/freeflowctl status
freeflowctl model e4b|e2b|<id>     # change the cleanup model
freeflowctl ane on|off             # speech-to-text on the Neural Engine or the GPU
freeflowctl screen-words on|off    # spell on-screen words
freeflowctl uninstall              # remove it from login
