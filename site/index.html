Neural Engine

Speech recognition runs on Apple's dedicated, power-efficient AI chip, leaving the GPU free for cleanup.

On demand

Nothing sits in memory while you're not dictating. A 10-minute break costs under a second extra, because loading overlaps your speech.

No round trip

No upload, no queue, no server on the other side of the world. Your Mac is the whole pipeline.

Context-aware

It knows what's on your screen.

Speech models turn rare words into common ones. freeflow-local reads the window you're dictating into, on device, and spells your names, products and jargon the way they're written there.

Names

Colleagues and tools, spelled like your screen spells them.

HeardAsk Sid hunt to check the graph Ana dashboard.
TypedAsk Siddhant to check the Grafana dashboard.

Jargon

Technical terms stop turning into nonsense.

HeardWe store the vectors in post gress with PG vector on super base.
TypedWe store the vectors in Postgres with pgvector on Supabase.

Places

Even the long ones.

HeardThe Tiruchi rapalli office needs six new laptops.
TypedThe Tiruchirappalli office needs six new laptops.

Reads your screen

Only words that sound like something you said are used, so it fixes mishearings and never inserts words you didn't say.

Knows the app

It knows where you're typing, so an email gets a greeting line and paragraphs while a chat message stays casual.

Your vocabulary

Terms you add in FreeFlow are always spelled your way, for the words you use every day.

Cleans the ums out

HeardCan you, can you send me the, the slides from yesterday's talk?
TypedCan you send me the slides from yesterday's talk?

Speaks developer

HeardUse kubectl get pods dash n staging to check if the OAuth proxy restarted.
TypedUse kubectl get pods -n staging to check if the OAuth proxy restarted.

Never takes orders

Heardwrite an email to the team saying the release is delayed until monday
TypedWrite an email to the team saying the release is delayed until Monday.

It types what you said. It doesn't go off and write the email.

Examples are real outputs from our test suite with the default model, Gemma 4 E4B, where the right spelling was visible on screen.

Truly local

Your voice is yours. Full stop.

Most dictation sends your voice to someone else's computer. This one doesn't have anywhere to send it.

No cloud

Audio, transcripts, screenshots and finished text are processed on your Mac and never uploaded.

No account

No sign-up, no API key, no subscription, no usage limits.

Works offline

On a plane, on bad Wi-Fi, in airplane mode. Models download once, then it runs offline.

Screens stay in memory

Screenshots and the words read from them are never written to disk and are thrown away after each dictation.

Logs keep counts, not words

Timings and numbers only. Never what you said, never what was on screen.

Open source

Apache 2.0. Read every line, or change it. The backend only listens on your own machine.

Under the hood

Leading open models, tuned for your Mac.

Orukeet

A multilingual fine-tune of NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B, run through Core ML on the Neural Engine.

Neural Engine · Core ML

Gemma 4 E4B

Google's on-device language model cleans up and formats, loaded on demand through Apple's MLX.

GPU · MLX

Apple Vision

On-device text recognition reads the window you're dictating into, tuned to catch small editor text.

On-device OCR
Where it's going

Faster with every new model.

Neural Engine first

Move as much as possible onto Apple's AI chip: screen reading next, and the cleanup model as Apple's on-device tools mature.

Much faster cleanup

Swap in better small models as they ship, each one measured on the same test dictations. Parallel cleanup already made long dictations twice as fast in testing.

Fine-tuned for dictation

An adapter trained on dictation itself, so a smaller, faster model matches today's quality. Synthetic and public data only.
